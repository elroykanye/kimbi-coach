# Exam answer verification protocol

Use this protocol for exam-like accounting answers, including generated answer keys.

## 1. Confirm permitted assistance

Classify the task as practice, past-paper review, mock examination, permitted take-home work, or potentially live/restricted assessment. For potentially restricted work, provide concept teaching or an analogous practice problem until the assistance rules are clear.

## 2. Build a provenance map

For each material input or rule, identify one of:

- **Question fact:** explicitly supplied in the assessment.
- **Course source:** supplied notes, textbook extract, syllabus, rubric, or instructor material. Retain the page or section when available.
- **External authority:** current primary or authoritative source, with jurisdiction and effective date.
- **Derived result:** computation or logical conclusion from identified inputs.
- **Assumption:** necessary but not given; state how it affects the answer.

Model memory alone is not an external source. Never present an uncited recollection as source verification.

## 3. Recheck independently

Use at least one genuinely independent check appropriate to the problem:

- recompute from the original facts without copying intermediate values;
- use an alternative method or reverse calculation;
- reconcile journal entries, ledgers, control accounts, schedules, and statements;
- test the accounting equation and debit-credit equality;
- check dates, units, signs, bases, rates, periods, rounding, and classification;
- test an edge case or counterexample;
- compare the conclusion with the cited authority and its scope or exceptions.

For a generated exam or answer key, solve every question before release and check that the prompt contains enough information for the stated answer.

## 4. Adversarial self-review

Try to falsify the answer. Check for:

- a plausible alternative interpretation;
- an omitted exception or condition;
- the right number produced for the wrong reason;
- arithmetic or transcription errors;
- unsupported certainty;
- conflict between the narrative, calculations, and final answer;
- a source that is outdated, secondary when primary authority is needed, or outside the correct jurisdiction.

## 5. Assign a confidence label

- **Verified:** all material claims have traceable support, current claims were checked against appropriate authority, independent checks passed, and no material ambiguity remains.
- **Reasoned but not externally verified:** calculations or reasoning passed internal checks, but an important external authority or supplied source could not be verified.
- **Uncertain:** missing facts, conflicting sources, unresolved ambiguity, or failed checks could materially change the answer.

Do not use “verified,” “trustworthy,” “definitive,” or equivalent language for the latter two categories.
