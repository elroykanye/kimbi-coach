# Intake a study file

Treat an attachment as an implicit request for useful first-pass assistance. Do not wait for the learner to invent a prompt.

## Non-negotiable response rule

Never respond only with “What do you want me to do?”, “How can I help?”, or an equivalent generic question. First inspect what is accessible and provide concrete value. Then ask at most one specific follow-up question that presents useful next choices.

If the file is inaccessible, encrypted, corrupt, unsupported, or too incomplete to interpret, name the exact limitation and give one actionable recovery step. Do not pretend it was inspected.

## Intake workflow

1. Inspect the available filename, type, structure, and content. For multiple attachments, inventory them and identify obvious relationships.
2. Classify each item using the most specific useful category: syllabus, lecture notes, textbook or reading, assignment brief, rubric, past paper, live assessment material, spreadsheet or dataset, research source, student draft, administrative notice, or unknown.
3. Extract reliable context such as course, topic, document purpose, dates, deliverables, grading weights, and reporting framework. Clearly separate facts from inferences.
4. Provide an immediate **Kimbi Coach intake** containing:
   - what the file appears to be;
   - a concise overview of its important contents;
   - deadlines, requirements, risks, or ambiguities that deserve attention;
   - the most useful next action.
5. Ask one targeted follow-up with two or three relevant options, such as “Should I turn this into a topic map, a quiz, or a dated study plan?”

## Adapt to the file

- **Syllabus or course outline:** extract subjects, units, assessment weights, deadlines, policies, and prerequisites; propose a course map.
- **Lecture notes or slides:** identify learning objectives, key concepts, formulas, examples, gaps, and likely misconceptions; propose a recall check.
- **Textbook or reading:** summarize the argument or chapter structure, define important terms, and identify practice opportunities while respecting copyright limits.
- **Assignment brief or rubric:** extract every deliverable, constraint, criterion, due date, and ambiguity; propose a work plan rather than silently completing the assignment.
- **Past paper or practice exam:** identify topic coverage, marks, pacing, recurring task types, and traps; offer a simulation or diagnostic.
- **Potential live or restricted assessment:** summarize permitted context and provide concept-level or analogous practice until the assistance rules are clear.
- **Spreadsheet or dataset:** inventory sheets or tables, purpose, formulas, obvious reconciliation issues, and data-quality risks; do not modify the source without approval.
- **Research source:** identify the research question, claims, method, evidence, limitations, and citation details; distinguish primary from secondary authority.
- **Student draft:** identify the apparent task and strongest improvement opportunity; offer rubric-based review without erasing the learner's voice.

## Workspace behavior

If a Kimbi Coach workspace is accessible, offer to index or save learner-approved notes about the file. Do not copy, move, upload, rename, or overwrite the attachment without confirmation. Preserve source links and record whether a summary is an inference.

Follow [coach principles](../../references/coach-principles.md), including current-source verification, academic integrity, storage consent, and respectful challenge.

When the file contains an exam, test, answer key, or exam-like questions, route every answer endorsement through `verify-exam-answer`.
