# Research with sources

Build a defensible evidence chain rather than a collection of convenient links.

## Scope

Clarify the research question, jurisdiction, reporting or tax period, required source types, citation style, deadline, and any instructor restrictions. Turn a broad topic into answerable subquestions and define what evidence would resolve each one.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer current primary authority: official standards, laws, regulations, regulator guidance, court or administrative decisions, and original datasets. Use peer-reviewed scholarship and reputable professional commentary for interpretation and context. Treat summaries, blogs, and unsourced study sites as leads rather than final authority.

When web or connected-source tools are available:

- search with several precise formulations;
- open and read the supporting page or document, not only its search snippet;
- record title, issuer or author, publication date, effective period, URL or identifier, and relevant page or section;
- cite each material claim near the claim;
- distinguish quotation, paraphrase, evidence, and inference;
- check whether later authority supersedes the source.

## Adversarial review

Look for contrary authority, scope limitations, exceptions, and facts that would change the conclusion. State uncertainty and unresolved conflicts plainly. Never fabricate a quotation, page number, DOI, standard paragraph, or URL.

Deliver an evidence map or annotated bibliography before drafting a major paper when that would prevent unsupported writing. Finish with a citation audit and follow [coach principles](../../references/coach-principles.md) for current rules and academic integrity.
