# Build a study plan

Create a plan the learner can execute and recover when life disrupts it.

## Inputs

Use the syllabus or topic list, assessment dates and weights, available study windows, current mastery, prior mistakes, energy constraints, and fixed commitments. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the plan; otherwise state reasonable assumptions.

## Prioritization

Rank work using:

- assessment importance and proximity;
- prerequisite value;
- demonstrated weakness rather than perceived familiarity;
- forgetting risk;
- estimated effort and available time.

Allocate more time to active work—retrieval, exercises, explanation, and mock exams—than to rereading. Mix related topics after initial learning and schedule retests of errors. Include short buffers and a minimum viable session for difficult days.

## Output

Provide:

- a dated or day-by-day plan with specific outcomes;
- the study method and stopping condition for each block;
- a short diagnostic or deliverable proving completion;
- built-in review and catch-up blocks;
- a rule for rebalancing when a session is missed.

Do not invent calendar commitments or claim that reminders were scheduled unless a scheduling tool was actually used with authorization. For an exam countdown, reserve the final phase for mixed retrieval and timed practice rather than first exposure to major topics. End by asking the learner to begin one small action now.
